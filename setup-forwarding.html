Setup

Finish this on your computer.

Mail rules live in your provider's settings, not in their phone app — Gmail's iOS app cannot create one at all. So this takes a browser, and about ninety seconds.

Step 1

What the rules do.

Seven rules, and this is all of them. Each one forwards a copy of matching mail to your VoyAIge address and leaves the original in your inbox, untouched. Nothing else is read, and nothing is installed anywhere.

They work on mail as it arrives. A Gmail rule never reaches back through mail you already have, so your existing inbox stays exactly as it is and this starts counting from the day you switch it on. If something already sitting in there matters — a flight next month, a bill due — forward it across by hand once and it will be picked up like any other.

    Start with one. You can add the others later, and you can delete any of them from your own settings at any time without telling us.

    Step 3

    Where your mail lives.

    1. Open Gmail on a computer → Settings (the gear) → See all settings → Forwarding and POP/IMAP.
    2. Click Add a forwarding address and paste the address from Step 2.
    3. Google sends a confirmation code to that address. It arrives here, not in your inbox — we read it out and show it to you below.
    4. Paste the code back into Gmail and click Verify. Leave the "Disable forwarding" option selected — the rules do the forwarding, not the global setting.
    5. Come back here and download the rule file, then in Gmail go to Filters and Blocked Addresses → Import filters.
    Why we show you the code instead of just clicking the link. Google's mail also carries a one-click confirmation link, and following it from our side would finish this in one hop. We do not: confirming a change to your Google account is an action your hand never took. It never acts on you, only for you.

    The rule file

    Download and import.

    Gmail will accept a rule pointing at an unconfirmed address and then forward nothing at all, so this stays closed until the code above has arrived.

    Apple only allows this from a browser, and only as a single rule. iCloud Mail has no filter import and no per-rule forwarding on iOS. So iCloud gets the simple version: forward the mail you want us to see, by hand or with one rule, to the address in Step 2. We would rather say that plainly than build a flow that half works.
    1. Open icloud.com/mail in a browser on a computer.
    2. Click the settings icon → Preferences → Rules → Add a Rule.
    3. Set the condition you want — a sender, or a word in the subject — and choose Forward to.
    4. Paste the address from Step 2 and save.
    5. Or skip all of that: forward individual emails to the address by hand. Everything on this page works the same way for mail you send yourself.
    Read this before you start. If your mail is a work or school Microsoft 365 account, your organisation very likely blocks forwarding to outside addresses at the tenant level. When it does, Outlook still lets you create the rule, still shows it as enabled, and silently forwards nothing — there is no error and no warning anywhere.

    That is a setting only an administrator can change (the anti-spam outbound policy). If the indicator below stays dark after you have made the rule, this is almost certainly why — it is not your rule, and it is not us. A personal outlook.com account has no such block.
    1. Open Outlook on the web → Settings → Mail → Rules.
    2. Add a rule, set the condition — subject contains, or a sender — and choose Forward to.
    3. Paste the address from Step 2 and save.
    4. Send yourself a test message that matches the rule and watch the indicator below.
